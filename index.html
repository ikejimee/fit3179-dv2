Australia's Great Outdoors

Which adventure activities Australians actually do — and where in the country they do them most. A look at national participation in 2025.

Bethany Aung · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Data: AusPlay (Australian Sports Commission)

Where Australians get outdoors

The map shades each state and territory by the share of adults who did the activity at least once in 2025. Bush walking leads almost everywhere, but the hotspots shift as you change activity — try switching to surfing or mountain biking.

Hover a state for its exact rate. Tasmania and the ACT punch well above their size for bush walking.

What leads the pack

Nationally, bush walking and cycling dominate the adventure category, while specialised pursuits like rock climbing and sailing remain niche.

The adventure gender gap

Most adventure activities skew male — cycling, fishing and mountain biking especially. Bush walking is the clear exception, where women participate slightly more than men.

Every activity, every state

Reading down a column shows a state's outdoor profile; reading across a row shows where an activity concentrates. Brighter cells mean higher participation.

Adventure across the ages

Participation peaks in different decades of life depending on the activity — bush walking holds up into middle age, while surfing and climbing fade faster.

About this visualisation

Domain: Adventure tourism & outdoor recreation in Australia. Audience: the average Australian adult curious about how the country plays outdoors and how their state compares.
